What the Certificate IV in Disability Support Covers

The Certificate IV in Disability Support is a nationally recognised qualification designed for people who want to work directly with individuals living with disability — in their homes, in community settings, or within specialist care organisations. It builds on the practical, everyday skills of frontline support work and adds the judgement, communication and leadership capability needed to work with less direct supervision.

Rather than treating disability support as a checklist of tasks, the course is grounded in a person-centred approach: understanding each individual's goals, preferences and strengths, and building support around what independence looks like for them. You'll learn to work respectfully within a person's own home, contribute to individualised plans, and help coordinate care alongside families, allied health professionals and support teams.

It's a strong fit whether you're already working in the sector and want a recognised qualification behind your experience, or you're moving into community services from another career and want a structured, practical pathway in.

What You'll Learn

Across a mix of core and elective units, you'll build capability across five key areas of disability support practice.

01

Person-centred practice

Following and contributing to individualised support plans built around a person's own goals and preferences.

02

Complex needs support

Providing safe, respectful support to people with disability who have higher or more complex support needs.

03

Community participation

Helping people build connections, access services and take part confidently in community and social life.

04

Behaviour support

Understanding and responding to behaviours of concern using established, person-centred behaviour strategies.

05

Health & wellbeing monitoring

Recognising changes in a person's physical or mental wellbeing and knowing when and how to escalate concerns.

06

Working without direct supervision

Building the judgement and reporting skills needed to work independently and, over time, coordinate a small team.
